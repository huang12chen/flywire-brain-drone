🌍① How to Open the World

No installation, no internet — just double-click a file.

  1. Open the web folder inside the project directory.
  2. Double-click web\world.html and your browser (Chrome / Edge recommended) will open the 3D microscopic world directly.
  3. On entry, a fruit fly 🪰 is automatically released. The "God Console" appears on the right — you can start experimenting right away.
📎 Dependency files (please keep the web\ directory structure intact, including the vendor\ subdirectory):
vendor\three.min.js (3D engine), snn_data.js (trained SNN weights & topology), snn_runtime.js (browser-side spiking inference engine), scene_fx.js (refined models & four-scene effects), brain_panel.js (🧠 Fruit Fly Brain Panel).
Missing any of these will break the experience (only a panel or blank background will remain). Do not move world.html on its own or scatter the directory structure.

🧠 Bottom-Left Fruit Fly Brain Panel — Real Connectome Live Firing (New in v5, Must-See)

Click "🧠 Brain → 🧠 Fruit Fly Brain Panel" in the top-right console to toggle on/off (button turns green = on; a "Close" button is also in the panel's top-left corner). It displays the real connectome used for training (FlyWire topology, 1,871 neurons / 45,524 edges) with millisecond-by-millisecond spike replay across a 12 ms decision window:

Panel ElementHow to Read It
👁️ LPLC2 Visual 210 Dot MatrixCompound-eye visual early-warning neurons (210 of them), sorted by preferred azimuth — whichever direction the threat comes from lights up the corresponding column first.
🌬️ JO Wind 325 Dot MatrixAirflow/wind receptors at the antenna base (325 of them), sorted by sensitive-axis direction; when threat airflow presses in, the corresponding direction lights up.
☁️ Interneurons 1165 (128 sampled)Intermediate circuits between LPLC2/JO and GF (1,165 total; 128 representative dots are drawn to fit).
⚡ GF ×2 Large DotsGiant Fibers — the escape command neurons, 2 of them. Bright = firing this millisecond.
🎯 Downstream 169 Dot MatrixGF downstream motor circuits (169 of them), converting the "escape" command into body-movement direction codes.
Flow Bands + Green NumbersReal synaptic connections between groups; the number = actual edge count (e.g., "LPLC2→GF direct 127", "Interneuron→GF 535"); when a spike passes through, green particles run along the band (spikes light up one by one).
📈 GF Membrane Potential CurveBottom graph: horizontal axis = 12 ms steps of the decision window, vertical axis = GF membrane potential (neural voltage); two lines = GF1 (cyan) / GF2 (purple); gold dashed line = Vth threshold (1.00). Curve crosses the line = GF firing = ⚡ Escape Triggered (⚡ is marked at the crossing point on the graph); the entire line staying below the dashed line = resting.
Top Status RowShows which replay step is playing (t = n/12 ms), loom / wind pressure readings, and either "⚡ GF Firing! Escape" or "Resting". With no threat / in manual mode, it displays "Awaiting threat decision…".
💡 The panel is a read-only bypass recorder: it "copies" each step's firing and potential snapshots from the SNN engine for visualization, without participating in or altering any computation (when the panel is closed, the SNN calculation path is bit-identical). Decisions occur roughly every 40 ms, each covering a 12 ms window; the panel replays the latest window at its own pace, making it easy to see how spikes propagate from the eyes to the giant fiber.
💡 The small toast messages that occasionally appear at the bottom of the screen are event announcements; when the large "⚡ Escape!" text appears at the center, it means the GF has fired and an escape maneuver has been initiated. Neural readings display "— / Not Running" in manual mode or when there is no active threat (no decision is in progress at that moment) — this is normal. When multiple threats arrive simultaneously, the decision responds to the nearest threat first (a simplification in this world); statistics still track threats individually.

🔬④ Experiments (This Is a Verification Testbed, Not a Scenic Tour)

Every button corresponds to an experimental variable in the report — playing = running experiments.

🧍➡️🚁 Body-Swap Experiment (Experiment Matrix D: Fly Body vs. Drone Body)

After selecting an entity, click "Fly Body / Drone Body" — same brain, new body:

  • 🪰 Fly Body: Lightweight, explosive acceleration, high top speed;
  • 🚁 Drone Body: Has inertia, limited acceleration, slightly "heavier" feel.

This lets you intuitively see: the same escape decision, housed in a body with different dynamics, produces different trajectories and handling. Honest caveat (REPORT Section 3.4): within the short burst windows of surprise threats, the two bodies' success rates are nearly identical (fly 0.698 vs. drone 0.694, physical integration metric — see REPORT 3.4) — the gap is not in neural decision-making but in execution dynamics; truly "dodging" requires earlier warning + greater maneuvering power. This is precisely what the "bionic brain + drone body" combination aims to address.

🎯 Threat Spawning (Related to Experiment Matrix C: Surprise Attack Speed)

Red Lunge Ball / Frog Tongue Flick / Dragonfly Dive attack speeds are randomized within approximately 3–9 m/s (this world's parameters: ball 3.5–9.0, frog 5.0–8.0, dragonfly 3.0–8.0 m/s; no low-speed tier). The miss distance range is controlled by the "Precise ↔ Relaxed" slider (New in v5; default Precise 0–1.2 cm, meaning tight and nearly every one scores; dragging to Relaxed restores 0–4.5 cm, producing more near-miss false alarms) — some threats will actually hit (scored rounds), some are just near-misses (if the fly jumped at them, it counts as 1 "false alarm"). Spamming "Auto Threats ×10" lets you stress-test at high intensity. Strict low/medium/high-speed comparisons (Experiment Matrix C) are in REPORT Section 3.3 (generated by evaluation scripts).

📊 Run 100 Times Statistics (Same Algorithm as Training Metrics)

Click "Run 100 Times Statistics" and the program generates 100 random threat scenarios in the background, outputting four metrics:

【Scene Name】100 Scenarios: Trigger x% Success y% Direction z° Latency w ms

  • Trigger Accuracy: The proportion of correct judgments on "is there a threat, should it jump" (based on whether the GF fires);
  • Obstacle Avoidance Success Rate: Among threats that meet trigger conditions (approaching, time-to-collision < 50 ms, distance < 25 cm, regardless of miss distance), the proportion where "GF fires and directional error < 30°" (aligned with the evaluation algorithm in train_snn.py);
  • Directional Error: The average angle between the escape direction and "directly away from the threat" (averaged over all valid threat samples, including non-firing rounds — this metric differs from the HUD's directional error);
  • GF Latency: Among the above threats, for rounds where the GF actually fires, the average milliseconds from decision onset to first firing (non-firing rounds are excluded).
💡 The statistics use the current scene's visual gain: switch to "Dark Night" and run 100 times to see performance degradation under weak vision (the Experiment B weak-vision condition, one click away). Note: the wind-disturbance mechanism of "Strong Wind" is not modeled in this simplified statistics (in-world Strong Wind only blows the body; it does not enter the statistics); for rigorous Strong Wind data, refer to REPORT Section 3.2.

❓⑤ Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Why doesn't wind pressure take effect in "Pure Vision" mode? Is it a bug?
A: Not a bug — it is deliberately disabled for the controlled experiment. "Pure Vision" is a modality control condition: to quantify how much vision alone can achieve, the wind sensory input channel is completely shut off, so wind pressure readings do not participate in decisions. Conversely, "Pure Wind" (wind_only) shuts off the vision channel entirely. The 3D world (world.html) defaults to Fusion mode; modality comparisons are done at training / evaluation time. For a rough in-browser modal comparison, open the legacy sandbox index.html in the same directory (it has Pure Vision / Pure Wind dropdowns).
Q2: How is the success rate calculated?
A: Be careful — there are three different metrics; do not mix them up: ① On-screen cumulative "success rate" (top-left HUD) = among scored rounds (miss distance < threat radius + 0.55 cm + 1.2 cm margin, meaning "a real collision is possible"), the proportion where "the entity jumped and was not ultimately hit" — does not check directional angle; ② "Run 100 Times" and report "Obstacle Avoidance Success Rate" = among "threats meeting trigger conditions" (approaching, time-to-collision < 50 ms, distance < 25 cm, regardless of miss distance), the proportion where "GF fires and escape directional error < 30°"; ③ The report's Category D "Body-Swap Experiment" uses a physical integration metric (both bodies' motion is simulated millisecond-by-millisecond, safe = minimum distance > sum of radii — see REPORT 3.4). These numbers are not the same and must not be mixed. False alarms are counted separately: threats that fail to meet the scoring threshold (miss distance ≥ threat radius + 0.55 cm + 1.2 cm margin) but still triggered a jump are counted as "false alarms" and do not enter the success rate. (Edge case: threats that miss the scoring threshold will not "end your run" even if they physically hit you in this world; but if you jumped at them, 1 "false alarm" is still recorded.)
Q3: What happens when the fruit fly is killed? Do I need to retrain?
A: Being hit simply means "that life is over" — it automatically respawns = factory reset (position and velocity are reset); the instinctual weights remain intact (the SNN is a fixed topology + trained weights), so retraining is completely unnecessary. (Edge case: threats that miss the scoring threshold won't "end your run" even if they hit you — see Q2.)
Q4: Can I drag the mouse to rotate the camera?
A: The current version auto-follows the selected entity (third-person), with scroll-wheel zoom only; click-drag rotation is not yet implemented (clicking and dragging an entity to reposition it is a separate, already-supported feature).
Q5 (New in v5): How do I read the 🧠 Brain Panel? What are those blinking dots?
A: The panel displays the real connectome (1,871 neurons / 45,524 edges) with millisecond-by-millisecond spike replay across a single 12 ms decision window. Dot matrix: each dot = one neuron, bright = firing this millisecond — top-left LPLC2 Visual 210 (sorted by preferred azimuth; the column matching the threat direction lights up first), bottom-left JO Wind 325, center Interneurons 1165 (128 sampled for display), center-right GF ×2, right Downstream 169. Flow bands with green particles = spikes propagating between groups (numbers are real synaptic edge counts). Bottom curve: horizontal axis = 12 ms steps, vertical axis = GF membrane potential, two lines = two GFs, gold dashed line = Vth threshold (1.00); curve crosses the line = GF firing = ⚡ Escape Triggered (⚡ is marked at the crossing), entire line below dashed = resting. The panel is just a "bypass meter" — it does not change any SNN values (closing it leaves the calculation path bit-identical).
Q6 (New in v5): What does the "Miss Distance Range" slider (Precise ↔ Relaxed) do?
A: It controls the miss distance range for newly spawned threats (how far off-target the threat is when launched). Precise (default) = 0–1.2 cm: nearly every threat is a scored round, ideal for stress-testing escape behavior. Dragging to Relaxed = 0–4.5 cm: more "near-miss" false alarms (if the fly jumps at a false alarm, it counts as a "false alarm"). It only affects threats spawned after the adjustment, not those already in flight. The slider changes the attack method, not brain values — the SNN and evaluation metrics remain unchanged.
Q7 (New in v5): Previously it said "scenes are just recolors" — has that changed?
A: As of v5, the four scenes have substantive differences: Daylight Meadow = grass waves + cloud shadows + pollen; Dark Night = moon + starry sky + 140 fireflies flickering and drifting (cool-toned moonlight); Storm = 700 slanting rain segments + high-speed wind streaks + wildly whipped grass + body blown off course by wind; Strong Wind = 520 airflow particles following the flow field (turning "wind" into visible streamlines). Lighting also differs by color temperature (warm daylight vs. cool moonlight) with shadow maps. Note that scenes still affect visual gain per Experiment Matrix B standards (Sunny 1.0 / Dark Night 0.3 / Storm 0.85), so switching scenes when running statistics = changing experimental conditions.

📜⑥ Scientific Notes (Honest Disclaimers)

This world is a scientific verification testbed, not a game product. The following limitations are consistent with Section 4 of REPORT.md (items 1, 2, and 5 are quoted here):

1. Training data is synthetic cues (generated from physics formulas), not electrophysiological or behavioral measurements; conclusions only hold for this specific control task.

2. The connectome comes from a single female adult fly; synaptic counts are anatomical connectivity proxies, not physiological weights.

5. The fruit fly is "pure instinct": no memory, no online learning (intentionally no online plasticity, to ensure reproducibility).

For the complete list of limitations (including weak pure-wind modality, manual semantic acceptance experience, etc.), see Section 4 of REPORT.md in the project root; training pipeline and local runtime environment are documented in README.md in the project root.
